Artists & Illustrators through the decades

By Ramsha Vistro | Wed 2nd Sep 2026

From our first issue in 1986 to our landmark 500th edition, discover the story of Britain’s favourite art magazine

When Artists & Illustrators published its first issue in October 1986, its purpose was clear: to inspire people to make art. Four decades and 500 issues later, that mission remains unchanged. While the tools, techniques and technologies available to artists have evolved, the magazine has continued to champion creativity through practical tuition, expert insight and an unwavering belief that every artist, whatever their experience, can keep learning.

From the outset, this magazine distinguished itself by placing practising artists at its heart. Over the years, readers have learned directly from internationally renowned painters, respected tutors and some of Britain’s most accomplished artists. Watercolour master Alvaro Castagnet has shared his distinctive approach to capturing atmosphere and light, while Adebanji Alade has guided readers through portraiture, drawing and painting with the same infectious enthusiasm that has made him one of Britain’s best-known artists. Drawing authority Jake Spicer has helped countless readers strengthen their observational skills through his accessible tutorials, while acclaimed artists including Al Gury, Terence Clarke and Sarah Jane Moon have each contributed demonstrations and professional insight, reflecting the magazine’s long-standing commitment to learning from those who are at the top of their craft.

Practical advice has always been the thread running through every issue. Thousands of step-by-step demonstrations, studio visits, product reviews and artist interviews have encouraged readers to experiment with new materials, master unfamiliar techniques and develop their own creative voice. Rather than simply celebrating finished paintings, Artists & Illustrators has consistently explored the process behind them, giving readers the confidence to pick up a brush, pencil or palette knife and create work of their own.

One of the magazine’s most enduring legacies is the evolution of its flagship competition. Launched in 2008 as Artists of the Year, it was created to provide emerging artists with a platform to exhibit their work and gain national recognition. Today, as the British Art Prize, it has grown into one of the UK’s leading art competitions, attracting thousands of entries each year and culminating in a public exhibition of shortlisted works. The competition has become about far more than winning. Finalists receive valuable exposure, while winners benefit from editorial coverage, industry recognition, gallery exhibitions and opportunities that can help transform a passion into a profession – not to mention a massive prize fund! Esteemed judges, including Alvaro and Adebanji, continue to lend their expertise, reinforcing the prize’s reputation for artistic excellence.

That commitment extends beyond the printed page. Through the British Art Club, online tutorials, editorial coverage, competitions and opportunities for readers to share their own work, Artists & Illustrators has built a creative community that supports artists at every stage of their journey. For many, the magazine has been more than a source of inspiration; it has been a trusted companion, encouraging them to develop their skills, discover new artists and take the next step in their creative lives.

500 issues is a remarkable publishing milestone, but it is also a celebration of the artists, readers and contributors who have shaped the magazine’s story since 1986. As Artists & Illustrators looks to the future, one thing remains certain: there will always be another technique to master, another artist to discover and another blank page waiting to be filled.
