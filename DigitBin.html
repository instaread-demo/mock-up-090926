Google finally explained the Pixel 11 Pro RAM cut, and the number behind it is wild

  • by Kushal Azza
  • September 8, 2026
  • Android
  • ✓ Reviewed by Nikhil Azza

The base Pixel 11 Pro ships with 12GB RAM instead of 16GB. Here is Google's own explanation and the memory pricing behind it.

The Pixel 10 Pro shipped with 16GB of RAM no matter which storage tier you picked. The Pixel 11 Pro does not, and that single change has generated more Reddit arguments than almost anything else about this year’s phone.

The 256GB Pixel 11 Pro and Pro XL ship with 12GB of RAM. Getting back to 16GB means buying the 512GB or 1TB model instead, on a phone that already costs 100 dollars more than last year’s equivalent.

Pixel 11 Pro RAM-cut complaints began within hours of the August 12 announcement, and Google has since provided a clear explanation rather than staying quiet about it.

TL;DR: Google’s VP of Devices and Services, Shakil Barkat, attributed the RAM cut to a supplier-driven memory shortage, citing Morgan Stanley figures showing 1GB of RAM jumped from about $2.80 to $12 in a year. The cut only affects the 256GB Pixel 11 Pro and Pro XL. Most owners report no noticeable slowdown in daily use, though Gemini Nano’s on-device processing has less headroom to work with.

What Google actually said about the Pixel 11 Pro RAM cut

Google did not dodge the question on the 4GB drop in RAM. Shakil Barkat, Google’s Vice President of Devices and Services, pointed directly to a supplier-driven memory shortage as the cause.

He further cited Morgan Stanley figures showing the cost of 1GB of RAM climbed from roughly $2.80 in 2025 to about $12 this year.

A company spokesperson framed the broader response in similar terms, saying Google is aggressively engineering through the supply challenge rather than absorbing the full cost increase silently, according to a report by PhoneArena.

That framing does not erase the frustration, but it does explain the timing. A RAM shortage that quadruples component cost in a year changes what a phone maker can include at a given price point without the sticker shock becoming unworkable.

How Reddit actually reacted, and it was not what I expected

A thread on r/GooglePixel captured the mood better than any spec sheet could. A Pixel 10 Pro owner weighing a free carrier upgrade to the base Pixel 11 Pro explained why they were leaning toward saying no, citing the 4GB RAM loss alongside a swapped-out thermometer sensor as reasons the free upgrade felt like a downgrade.

What surprised me reading through the replies was how split the sentiment actually was. A poll on the RAM question found most respondents comfortable with 12GB as a baseline, with roughly 40 percent selecting it as their personal minimum requirement for a smartphone.

That gap between the loudest complaints and the quieter majority opinion is worth sitting with. The people angriest about losing 4GB of RAM are not necessarily representative of how most Pixel 11 Pro buyers will actually feel a month into ownership.

Does 12GB memory actually cause problems in daily usage?

For messaging, browsing, and streaming video, 12GB is enough headroom that most people will not notice a practical difference from 16GB. That was true on the Pixel 10 series too, where 16GB was already generous for typical daily use.

Where it could matter is heavier multitasking, video editing, or gaming sessions that keep several demanding apps resident in memory at once. Those workloads are the ones most likely to trigger app reloads sooner on 12GB than they would on 16GB.

Gemini Nano, Google’s on-device AI model, is the other consideration. Less available memory means less headroom for local AI processing to run without falling back to cloud-based responses, though Google has not published specific benchmarks showing how much this affects response quality or speed on the entry configuration.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does every Pixel 11 Pro have less RAM than the Pixel 10 Pro?

No. Only the entry 256GB Pixel 11 Pro and Pro XL dropped to 12GB. The 512GB and 1TB versions still ship with 16GB, matching what the Pixel 10 Pro offered across every tier.

Why did Google cut RAM instead of just raising the price further?

Google cited a supplier-driven memory shortage that pushed RAM component costs up roughly four times in a year, according to Morgan Stanley figures the company referenced directly.

Will 12GB of RAM actually slow down my Pixel 11 Pro?

For typical daily use, most owners report no noticeable difference. Heavier multitasking or on-device AI processing has less headroom, though Google has not published specific performance data on the gap.

Is it worth paying extra for the 512GB Pixel 11 Pro just for 16GB RAM?

Only if you also need the additional storage. Buying a larger storage tier solely to recover 4GB of RAM is a more expensive way to solve a problem that may not affect your actual usage.

Are other phone makers cutting RAM the same way this year?

The memory shortage cited by Google affects the broader industry, and several manufacturers have raised prices in 2026 partly for the same component cost reasons, though not every brand has cut RAM specifically.

Is it worth choosing the Pixel 11 Pro with 12GB?

If your Pixel 11 Pro usage looks like messaging, photos, and casual browsing, the 12GB entry model is unlikely to feel different from 16GB in any way you would actually notice.

If you multitask heavily or want maximum headroom for Gemini Nano specifically, the jump to 512GB buys back the RAM Google trimmed, at a real cost premium that has nothing to do with the extra storage itself.
