Gibson wraps the SJ-200 and Hummingbird in koa

Words by Mixdown

Part of the Custom Artisan Series, both pair koa back and sides with a AAA Sitka spruce top and L.R. Baggs electronics, in right- and left-handed Honey Burst.

Gibson has given two of its most recognisable acoustics a koa makeover, releasing the SJ-200 Elegant Koa and Hummingbird Elegant Koa as part of its Custom Artisan Series. Both pair koa back and sides with a AAA Sitka spruce top, both wear a Honey Burst finish, and both come in right- and left-handed versions.

Catch up on all the latest news here.

Koa is a Hawaiian tonewood prized as much for its figured grain as its sound, and it tends to start out bright and focused before warming and filling out the more it’s played. Against the Sitka top, Gibson describes a rich, articulate voice that gains complexity over time. The two models come with abalone inlays and decorative detailing, finished to Custom Shop standard. The figured koa and abalone make them as much a display piece as a player. L.R. Baggs electronics are fitted throughout, so either guitar is ready to plug in for a stage or a session without an aftermarket pickup.

Both acoustics are among Gibson’s most famous shapes. The SJ-200, the original “King of the Flat-Tops”, is the big-bodied jumbo with the low end and presence to fill a room, while the Hummingbird is the square-shouldered dreadnought that you’ll find is behind countless records since the ’60s. Koa suits both, adding a little top-end sparkle to shapes better known for body and warmth.

Roland’s FP-40 is its first piano with onboard Wi-Fi

Words by Mixdown

The latest FP-series digital piano runs Roland's Piano Reality sound engine and PHA-4 keyboard, and links to the Skoove-built Piano Sphere app for lessons and real-time feedback.

Roland has launched the FP-40, its first piano with onboard Wi-Fi. It comes tied to Piano Sphere, a free learning app made with Skoove, and connects to your home Wi-Fi by itself, so Piano Sphere is ready to go the moment you switch the piano on.

FP-40 at a glance

  • Roland’s first digital piano with built-in Wi-Fi
  • Piano Reality Sound Engine, drawn from the GP-series grands
  • PHA-4 Standard keyboard – weighted hammer action, escapement, Ivory Feel keys, triple sensors
  • Redesigned speaker system with more low-end
  • Free Piano Sphere learning app, developed with Skoove
  • 100 licensed songs included, no subscription; upgrade for 1000+ songs and lessons
  • Real-time feedback through the flashing Piano Sphere button
  • Bluetooth Audio/MIDI and USB-C
  • Wireless updates, with playing history tracked even when the app is closed
  • Available in black or white

Catch up on all the latest news here.

The FP-40’s sound comes from the Piano Reality engine Roland uses in its GP-series grands, mixing samples with some modelling for the resonance and dynamics of an acoustic. The PHA-4 Standard keyboard has fully weighted hammer action, escapement and Ivory Feel keys, and its triple sensors track fast repeated notes more accurately, so it’s an action a beginner won’t grow out of quickly. A redesigned speaker layout adds low-end, so more of the playing comes through.

Piano Sphere is built on Skoove’s teaching method. It starts players on songs they already know and builds lessons around skill level, taste and pace, with step-by-step guidance. When a passage gives trouble, the Piano Sphere button on the piano flashes to offer help, and streaks and reward badges keep the practice habit going. The app is free with the piano and comes with 100 licensed pop and classical songs, no subscription needed, with over 1000 more songs and lessons available as an upgrade.

Because it’s online, the FP-40 logs playing time even when the app is closed, updates itself wirelessly and gains new features and content after purchase. Bluetooth and USB-C handle the rest of the connections, for streaming audio through the speakers, recording, or running the Roland Piano App to adjust the sounds. The cabinet comes in black or white.

“In our new midterm management plan, which began in 2026, we have positioned ‘Direct Connect’ as one of our key growth strategies,” said CEO Masahiro Minowa, whose stated aim is “connecting directly with every customer and supporting their individual musical journeys.”

For local enquiries, head to Roland Australia.
