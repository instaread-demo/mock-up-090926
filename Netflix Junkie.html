Some films arrive with the weight of a major studio behind them, while others build momentum through festivals and word of mouth. A Mosquito in the Ear belongs to the latter category, marking writer-director Nicola Rinciari’s feature directorial debut and bringing together Jake Lacy, Nazanin Boniadi, and newcomer Ruhi Pal. The comedy-drama premiered at the Santa Barbara International Film Festival in February before receiving a limited theatrical release in June 2026.

Now that the film has moved beyond its theatrical run, here’s where to watch A Mosquito in the Ear online.

Is A Mosquito in the Ear available to stream online?

A Mosquito in the Ear is currently available to watch at home in the U.S. through digital rental and purchase. Viewers can rent the film through Amazon Video and the Apple TV Store, with rental prices starting at $4.99. Those looking for a higher-quality viewing option can also rent the film in 4K for $5.99, where available. Both Amazon Video and Apple TV Store also offer the option to purchase the movie digitally for viewers who want to add it to their libraries.

At present, A Mosquito in the Ear does not have a free streaming option listed in the US. Currently, the movie is available as part of a subscription streaming service, meaning digital rental or purchase is the primary way to watch it at home.

As movie is gaining attention left and right, what is the movie about?

What is A Mosquito in the Ear about?

A Mosquito in the Ear is a 2026 drama from writer-director Nicola Rinciari, based on Andrea Ferraris’ graphic novel Una Zanzara nell’Orecchio. The film stars Jake Lacy as Andrew, Nazanin Boniadi as Daniela, and Ruhi Pal as Sarvari, a four-year-old girl they have adopted from India.

The story follows Andrew and Daniela as they travel to Goa to meet Sarvari and bring her home to the United States. What they expect to be the beginning of their life as a family quickly becomes more complicated when Sarvari struggles with leaving the orphanage she has always known as home.

The couple must navigate her distress while also confronting the challenges of becoming parents, including cultural and language barriers. At its core, the film explores adoption, parenthood, communication, and the difficult process of building trust between people who are still learning how to understand one another.
