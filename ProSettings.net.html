The ZOWIE G-SR-SE: History and Hands-On with the Orange II

Written by Lars | Published on Sep 1, 2026 | Last modified on Sep 2, 2026

Introduction

The ZOWIE G-SR-SE has been a staple in the pro gaming scene for a decade now, and while the brand continues to make improvements to the line, the core experience remains the same. In this article, we dive into what makes this pad such a success in the gaming scene, and talk a bit about the history and future of this classic.

History and Pro Scene Relevance

First things first: the G-SR-SE of today is not a recolor of the well-known G-SR. Instead, it’s a separate mousepad line that exists within ZOWIE’s product lineup.

It did, however, start off as a colored version of the G-SR (which, at the time, was referred to as the ‘G-SR Blue’ within the community). That pad was slightly faster than the G-SR due to the printed surface, and quickly gained a cult following, but that particular recolored version was quickly sold out, leaving fans to wonder when (and indeed if) ZOWIE would release that fan-favorite pad again.

The G-SR-SE as we now know it first saw the light of day in the form of a special-edition collaboration between ZOWIE and HLTV way back in 2016. This was the base for the current G-SR-SE, as it had a smoother (and thus faster) surface, and was no longer a simple (coated) recolor of the G-SR.

Some months after the limited HLTV release, the G-SR-SE Blue marked the official launch of the G-SR-SE as a separate, standalone product in ZOWIE’s lineup.

Ever since that release, the pad has been a staple in the pro scene.

Today, almost ten years after its release and now on its second major iteration, the G-SR-SE is the fifth most-used mousepad across all of our analyzed games. It’s used in all kinds of games ranging from BRs like Fortnite (5th place) to tactical shooters like CS2 (4th place) and can rightfully be called an iconic mousepad.

Different Versions

Over the years, we’ve seen many different versions of the G-SR-SE. We’re currently on the ‘II’ version but the engineers at ZOWIE also introduce smaller, more incremental improvements from time to time. Such was the case with the G-SR-SE Rouge back in 2022, which introduced a smoother surface, and better humidity resistance.

Changes between versions aren’t huge, though. That’s important for people who like consistency, and for fans of the glide that the G-SR-SE provides. Instead of a drastically different glide or overall mousepad design, you’ll notice improvements to things like the foam consistency and the base, for example.

The II version has been out for almost a year now, and we’ve seen four releases inside that line so far: the Rouge, the Bi, the Blue, and the newly released Orange. All feature a 3D-looking ZOWIE logo on the surface.

Pictured below: a collection of various G-SR-SE versions. From left to right: the G-SR-SE Blue, the G-SR-SE Divina Pink, the G-SR-SE Deep Blue, and the G-SR-SE II Orange.

Performance

The G-SR-SE has always been known as a very well-balanced pad. It’s not as fast as medium pads, but not overly muddy either. Thus, if you want a glide that’s decently smooth and quick once you’ve started a flick, but a bit more controlling when making small aiming movements, the G-SR-SE is a great choice.

Within ZOWIE’s own pad lineup (pictured below), the brand positions it as a middle-ground pad, too. In our own mousepad list, the latest versions are categorized as ‘control’ pads, with earlier iterations sometimes just edging above that and getting ranked as control-oriented pads.

In any case: what you’re getting is a glide that feels tailor-made for tactical shooters, but which doesn’t limit you to those specific games. The G-SR-SE, after all, isn’t a so-called ‘mud pad’ (a community name for an extremely controlling mousepad) so you don’t feel as if you’re fighting the pad with every small move you want to make. Tracking and flicking, for example, are aiming moves that feel more natural on the G-SR-SE than they do on hard control pads.

The Latest Orange II Version

The G-SR-SE Orange II is the latest chapter in the G-SR-SE story. In terms of performance and glide, it is the same as the Blue II and the Rouge II, but it does add a striking new colorway to the lineup.

Some people will lament the fact that there are no neutral pads in the G-SR-SE lineup, but there are usually enough options available out there to fit most setups. Additionally, the pads in their normal size aren’t huge (47×39 cm), meaning that they don’t necessarily become a centerpiece of a setup by taking up most of your desk. People who want to go for larger mousepads can also go for 50×50 versions, however.

Personal Experience

Control No More

For a couple of years, I had been a fan of heavy and soft control pads. However, for quite some time I felt inconsistent in my aim. I tried a multitude of other (heavy) control pads to see if I could find something I liked. Maybe I needed even more control to rein in my mouse so that my swipes and sprays would be more consistent? Maybe I needed a softer foam for more modularity? I tried a lot of options, but in the end, the G-SR-SE II turned out to be a great solution for my problems. And that’s for a number of reasons.

A More Consistent Experience

First off, the dense and consistent foam means that every swipe or micro-correction feels exactly the same. The glide itself also feels really good. I don’t feel ‘stuck’ when I have to make a small correction after a flick, but I also don’t feel as if I’m gliding on ice, either. For my current main mouse, it feels just about perfect.

Things like spraying in CS2 also feel quite natural due to the tight foam. No matter how tense or relaxed I am that day: my spray always feels the same, as the mouse surface doesn’t allow for a ton of ‘mouse feet sinkage’.

So, as it turns out I didn’t need a softer foam, or a more controlling pad. I needed a harder, more consistent one. You could note my inconsistency with softer foams down to user error, or maybe I grew out of them (for now) but all I can say is that I currently really enjoy the harder, bouncier foam of the G-SR-SE because I feel like my aim is a lot more reliable with it.

Before I went over to softer control pads, I actually mained pads quite like the G-SR-SE, so perhaps my short-lived preference for softer foams was just a phase, but I do know that I feel like I am performing more reliably on a day-to-day basis with the G-SR-SE. That’s not to say that it’s the solution for everyone who suffers with consistency, but for me, it seems like it’s the right pad right now.

Availability

One more thing that I really like is how available these pads are. If a pad wears down, or I damage one, I can very easily get a replacement from any major manufacturer within a day. It also won’t cost me an arm and a leg. Especially the availability aspect is something that can’t be said for some of the more niche pads out there, and I suspect factors like these also play a big part in pad popularity with the pros.

For me, it also means that I can keep an extra pad (and a spare) at a friend’s house where we usually come together to game, for example.

Closing Thoughts

Obviously, a person’s experience with a mousepad is subjective. And I won’t say that I will stick with the G-SR-SE forever, either. But for now, I can really see why a ton of pros swear by this pad, even with more specialized/premium alternatives on the market.

What’s Next?

We can’t predict the future. But, we can look at the past, and make an educated guess. Over the next few months, we fully expect ZOWIE to release a bunch of new colorways, for example. The Gris II would be a likely candidate to appear at some point, for instance. Personally, we also hope for some more collaborative releases. Team-specific versions, for example, would be cool. This wouldn’t be a first for ZOWIE (think of the Tyloo G-SR-SE) so it’s not something that’s completely unthinkable.

But whatever happens, we do suspect (and hope) that ZOWIE does not make any big changes to the overall experience of the pad. The G-SR-SE is a rock-solid workhorse of a mousepad, and we fully expect it to maintain its status as a very popular option over the next couple of years.
