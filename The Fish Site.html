    Certified Humane leads 2026 aquaculture welfare benchmark

    Freshwater fish Marine fish Molluscs +5 more
    9 September 2026, at 7:18 am · 3 min read

    Aquatic Life Institute’s fifth annual assessment identifies progress in operational welfare standards but finds that provisions supporting natural behaviour remain limited.

    Certified Humane has retained the highest score in Aquatic Life Institute’s annual assessment of animal welfare requirements within aquaculture certification schemes. The organisation awarded Certified Humane a score of 9.5 in its 2026 Aquaculture Certification Schemes Benchmark, followed by RSPCA at 8.45 and Aquaculture Stewardship Council (ASC) at 7.95.

    Certified Humane’s score increased following changes to its standards informed by recommendations in ALI’s 2025 assessment. Best Aquaculture Practices (BAP) demonstrated one of the largest improvements in overall score and continues to show a strong commitment to strengthening its animal welfare standards through ongoing engagement and standard development.

    “We are honored that Certified Humane has once again been recognised as the top-ranked scheme in the 2026 ALI's Aquaculture Certification Schemes Benchmark. This recognition underscores the importance of robust animal welfare standards in promoting higher welfare for farmed Atlantic salmon and driving meaningful improvements across the aquaculture sector,” said Rosangela Poletto, scientific committee director at Certified Humane, in a press release.

    The 2026 edition analyses current welfare requirements within the primary farming standards of 12 global seafood certification schemes and two international ratings agencies, representing the broadest scope of the Benchmark to date, expanding the geographic and regulatory diversity represented in the assessment.

    The schemes and agencies were assessed on requirements covering water quality, stocking density, environmental enrichment, feed composition, transport and handling and stunning and slaughter.

    The assessment also examined whether their standards prohibit octopus and cephalopod farming, insects in aquafeed, the certification of shrimp produced from eyestalk-ablated broodstock, genetically modified organisms used to accelerate growth and the unjustified use of antimicrobials.

    For the 2026 edition, ALI expanded its transport criterion to include routine handling practices. It also introduced antimicrobial misuse as a core prohibition, reflecting potential welfare concerns associated with routine, prophylactic or otherwise unjustified use.

    Behavioural welfare remains a weakness

    An overall assessment of all data collected showed that operational welfare is well established throughout the standards. Requirements relating to feed governance, water quality management, stocking density, transport and handling procedures, and humane stunning and slaughter are now incorporated by most certification schemes, providing an increasingly consistent baseline for animal welfare protection.

    However, behavioral welfare remains underdeveloped. Requirements promoting environmental complexity, behavioural stimulation, and opportunities for natural behaviour continue to be among the least frequently adopted provisions, indicating that positive welfare outcomes remain insufficiently addressed.

    An additional highlight from the 2026 Benchmark comes from the six certification schemes which now prohibit octopus or cephalopod farming: Certified Humane, RSPCA, ASC, Soil Association Organic, Friend of the Sea and BAP. ALI said this reflected growing opposition to the practice.

    “This year, we saw certification schemes engage with our findings, reconsider existing requirements, and, in some cases, make concrete changes or commitments in response,” said Steffan Edward, certification lead at ALI. “That is exactly what we hope this work can achieve.”

    The Benchmark assesses the content of animal welfare standards rather than their implementation or compliance. It does not assess environmental sustainability, food safety, social responsibility, governance performance, or the implementation of and compliance with the standards. 

    The full 2026 assessment is available through the Aquatic Life Institute website.
